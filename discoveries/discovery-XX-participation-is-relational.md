# Discovery XX — Participation Is Relational

## Status

Candidate.

Not yet canonized.

## Question

Is participation an ontological entity type, or is participation a
relationship/projection through which something becomes legible within a
biome?

## Existing Constraint

IGE establishes that participation is the act of projecting legible
state and is not membership.

Therefore participation must not be reduced to membership, identity, or
agency.

## Observation

The ontology loop distinguishes:

- actor — a distinction based on capacity to act;
- participant — a contextual designation;
- state — a distinguishable condition;
- projection — a mechanism through which state becomes legible;
- participation — a relationship through which something participates
  within a biome.

An actor may participate, but participation does not establish that
something is an actor.

Something may participate without agency.

An actor may exist without currently participating.

The same actor may participate differently across different biomes
without becoming a different actor.

## Candidate Conclusion

> Participation is provisionally understood as a relationship/projection
> rather than an ontological entity type.

Therefore:

> Participant is provisionally understood as a contextual designation
> arising from participation.

## Non-Equivalences

Participation is not:

- membership;
- agency;
- ownership;
- responsibility;
- identity.

Likewise:

- participant != actor;
- participation != membership;
- participation != ownership;
- participation != responsibility.

## Boundary

Participation occurs through a legitimate relationship across a
constitutional or biome boundary.

The participating thing does not thereby become owned by, identical to,
or subordinate to the biome in which it participates.

## Open Question

What is the ontological status of projection?

Specifically:

> Is projection an ontological capability, or is projection the boundary
> mechanism through which ontology becomes epistemically legible?

## Next Test

Stress-test projection before treating participation as established.
