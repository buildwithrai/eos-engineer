Per ADR-ECOSYSTEM-0001, an unowned canonical capability is an orphan.
An orphan violates ecosystem integrity.

OCS ADR-0001 declares OCS "is intended to become an Engineering
Operating System" with "future AI capabilities." OCS's own handbook
establishes the boundary discipline: "Deterministic Before
Probabilistic" and "Knowledge Before Intelligence"
(Facts -> Knowledge -> Reasoning -> Decisions). "AI augments
engineering; it does not replace engineering."

Discovery-55 established: Engineer survived the identity-vs-role axis
because of human origin of intent. Non-human cannot substitute.
EOS is non-human. EOS cannot claim the Engineer identity.

## Question

Is EOS a distinct participant identity, or a role any Participant can
exhibit?

If EOS is a role, it collapses — it is a behavior any Participant can
play, not a specialization.

If EOS is a distinct identity, it survives — it requires something
beyond Existence + Distinction + Projection to be distinguishable.

---

## Identity Resolution (Candidate)

EOS is a distinct identity.

Engineering judgment — probabilistic, evidence-gated, recorded as
declared/candidate state — is not a behavior any existing participant
can exhibit without violating its constitutional responsibility:

- IGE reconciles deterministically. It never invents.
- OCS measures deterministically. AI augments, does not replace.
- Omnia produces organizational reality.
- Corpus sustains continuity.

Judgment is therefore unowned responsibility. An unowned responsibility
is an orphan. EOS is the participant whose responsibility is judgment.

EOS is not Engineer. Engineer is distinguished by human origin of
intent. EOS is non-human and does not substitute for Engineer.

EOS is not OCS. OCS is the deterministic Engineering Operating System.
EOS is the probabilistic Engineering Operating Intelligence.

Division of responsibility with OCS, per OCS's own knowledge chain:

- OCS owns the canonical model: Facts -> Knowledge
  (Discover, Model, Analyze, Verify, Transform, Present, Persist)
- EOS owns Reasoning -> Decisions: probabilistic judgment over
  evidence, recorded as declared/candidate state

OCS observes EOS's projections the same way it observes any other
Participant. EOS consumes OCS's canonical model as evidence.

## Capability Ownership Resolution (Candidate)

Intelligence is an orphaned canonical capability.

Per ADR-ECOSYSTEM-0001, an orphan violates ecosystem integrity.

EOS owns Intelligence.

Classification (candidate, tested by the Experiment-38 procedure):

- Not Constitutional — Intelligence does not govern.
- Not Infrastructure — Intelligence does not execute.
- Not Domain — Intelligence does not produce business reality.
- Not Observational — Intelligence does not measure; OCS measures.
- Foundational — Intelligence is a base engineering capability, like
  Difference, upon which other capabilities build.

Ownership of Intelligence does not grant EOS ownership of its object.
EOS does not own projects, products, or observation. EOS owns judgment.

## Falsification Design

### F1 — Identity vs Role

Attempt to model EOS as a role.

If any existing participant (IGE, Omnia, OCS, Corpus) can exhibit
probabilistic engineering judgment recorded as declared/candidate state
without violating its constitutional responsibility, EOS collapses to a
role.

FALSIFIED if true.

### F2 — OCS Boundary

Attempt to have OCS absorb EOS's responsibility under ADR-0001's
"future AI capabilities."

If OCS can produce engineering judgment within its deterministic
pipeline without changing the canonical representation contract, EOS is
redundant.

FALSIFIED if true.

### F3 — Engineer Substitution

Attempt to substitute EOS for Engineer.

If EOS can fulfill Engineer's responsibility without human origin of
intent, EOS is not distinct from Engineer.

FALSIFIED if true.

### F4 — Classification Precedes Ownership

Re-run Experiment-38 with Intelligence added.

If Intelligence cannot be consistently classified independently of
ownership, Discovery-62 fails and this ownership resolution is invalid.

FALSIFIED if true.
