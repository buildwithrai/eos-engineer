# Constitutional Framework

EOS is an enabling framework for becoming within the engineering biome.

EOS is an IGE citizen. EOS is not a second IGE, nor is it a subordinate
implementation of IGE. IGE establishes ecosystem-level constitutional
conditions; EOS constitutes and evolves its own local biome within those
conditions.

EOS does not replace IGE, OCS, OW, Engineer, intelligence, agent, system,
or any other participant.

EOS is not an agent.
EOS is not intelligence.
EOS is not a system.
EOS is not an engineering tool.

Actors participate within EOS. An actor is distinguishable from a
participant by its capacity to act. Actor identity must not be collapsed
into human, agent, system, or intelligence identity unless the EOS
ontology later establishes such a distinction.

## Engineering

Engineering is not fundamentally defined by civil, industrial, software,
or other disciplinary boundaries. It is not defined by artifact,
technology, profession, or actor type.

At the fundamental level:

> Engineering is deliberate participation in transformation while
> remaining accountable to reality and constraint.

EOS therefore enables engineering becoming within its biome.

The purpose of EOS is not to prescribe what every actor must become.
Its purpose is to enable the engineering biome to remain capable of
becoming through difference while preserving coherence, epistemic
integrity, relationships, and traceability.

## IGE Citizenship

EOS is constituted within the IGE ecosystem while retaining its own
local identity and constitutional development.

IGE establishes ecosystem-level conditions for citizenship. EOS
establishes the local constitutional conditions of the engineering biome.

Conformance does not mean obedience, sameness, or duplication.

Therefore:

- conformance != obedience
- conformance != sameness
- citizenship != subordination
- difference != incoherence

IGE must preserve ecosystem coherence without requiring homogenization.

EOS may discover principles that are meaningful within EOS without
automatically promoting them to IGE-level principles.

Likewise, IGE-level principles may establish conditions of EOS
citizenship without determining EOS's complete local ontology,
epistemology, or relationships.

## Constitutional Boundaries

The EOS constitutional framework consists of five boundaries:

### constitution/

What must remain true for EOS to remain EOS?

### ontology/

What can exist and participate within the EOS biome?

### epistemology/

How does EOS distinguish what is known, observed, inferred, asserted,
decided, unresolved, or otherwise epistemically situated?

### relationships/

How do participants and actors relate while becoming?

### conformance/

How does EOS remain an IGE citizen while constituting and evolving its
own biome?

These boundaries define the framework. They do not prescribe an
implementation.

No EOS concept should be promoted to constitutional status merely
because it is useful to an implementation.
