# EOS Contracts

## Status

Derived domain.

Contracts are a constitutional-adjacent domain of EOS.

This document defines what an EOS contract is and distinguishes contracts
from ontology, relationships, epistemology, and implementation.

It does not constitute the EOS constitution.

It does not define the complete EOS contract set.

---

## 1. Contract Boundary

Ontology asks:

> What exists?

Relationships ask:

> How may existing things relate?

Contracts ask:

> What must remain true for a claimed relationship to remain coherent?

Epistemology asks:

> How can we know whether those conditions remain true?

Therefore:

    ontology
        ↓
    relationships
        ↓
    contracts
        ↓
    epistemology

This is a semantic distinction, not an implementation hierarchy.

The domains may inform one another.

---

## 2. What a Contract Is

An EOS contract is a condition of coherence governing a legitimate
relationship within the EOS biome.

A contract does not primarily prescribe behavior.

A contract establishes the conditions under which a relationship may
remain valid, coherent, or recognized.

Therefore:

> Contract is not command.

> Contract is not implementation.

> Contract is not ownership.

> Contract is not policy merely because policy may be expressed through
> contracts.

A contract establishes what must remain true when a relationship is
claimed.

---

## 3. Contract and Ontology

Ontology establishes the things and conditions that EOS can distinguish.

For example:

- entity
- actor
- participant
- identity
- distinction
- state
- possibility
- capability

A contract does not create these concepts.

Instead, contracts operate over relationships involving them.

Therefore:

    ontology
       ↓
    identifies
       ↓
    relationship
       ↓
    establishes
       ↓
    contract

A useful implementation does not become an ontological primitive merely
because a contract references it.

---

## 4. Contract and Relationship

A relationship describes a connection.

A contract establishes a condition of coherence for that connection.

For example:

    capability ──owned-by──> actor

is a relationship.

The statement:

> Canonical ownership of a capability is unique.

is a contract candidate.

Therefore:

    relationship = what connects
    contract     = what must remain true

The two must not be collapsed.

A relationship may be discovered before its contract is known.

A contract may also reveal that a proposed relationship is incoherent.

---

## 5. Contract and Epistemology

A contract establishes a condition.

It does not establish that the condition is true.

Epistemology determines how the truth of a contract condition can be
known, inferred, observed, demonstrated, or left unresolved.

Therefore:

    contract
       │
       │ condition
       ▼
    epistemology
       │
       │ knowledge of condition
       ▼
    evidence / observation / inference
       │
       ▼
    reconciliation
       │
       ▼
    judgment

A failed ability to establish a condition does not automatically prove
that the condition is false.

The distinction between:

- false;
- unobserved;
- unresolved;
- contradicted;
- inferred;

belongs to epistemology.

---

## 6. Contract and Implementation

Implementation is downstream of the contract.

An implementation may enforce a contract.

It does not define the contract merely by enforcing it.

For example:

    database constraint
    API validation
    type system
    runtime check
    workflow guard

may implement a contract.

None of these implementations constitute the contract itself.

The contract remains valid independently of the implementation used to
enforce or observe it.

No implementation concern should be promoted into a contract merely
because it is convenient to implement.

---

## 7. Candidate Contract: Identity and Distinction

Relationship:

    identity ↔ distinction

Candidate condition:

> A claimed identity must remain distinguishable from what it is not.

The contract does not require permanent sameness.

Identity may persist through change.

The requirement is maintained distinction, not absence of difference.

Potential incoherence occurs when a claimed identity can no longer be
distinguished from the identity it is being distinguished against.

The epistemic question of how such distinction is established remains
outside this contract.

---

## 8. Candidate Contract: Entity and Possibility

Relationship:

    entity ↔ possibility

Candidate condition:

> Possibility must not be represented as an enacted capability merely
> because it is possible.

Possibility precedes capability.

An entity may possess or participate within a possibility space without
having constituted a capability corresponding to every possibility.

Therefore:

    possibility ≠ capability

This contract preserves openness without treating every hypothetical
possibility as an established capability.

The epistemology of possibility remains unresolved.

---

## 9. Candidate Contract: Actor and Participation

Relationship:

    actor ↔ participation

Actor is distinguished by capacity to act.

Participation is enacted relationship.

Therefore:

    capacity ≠ enactment

Candidate condition:

> A claimed act of participation must have a distinguishable basis of
> agency.

The basis of agency is not yet constitutionally restricted to:

- human;
- agent;
- intelligence;
- system;
- engineer.

The complete agency ontology remains a derived concern.

Delegation, collective agency, system agency, and other forms remain open
to investigation.

---

## 10. Candidate Contract: Participant and Projection

Relationship:

    participant ↔ projection

Participation becomes legible through projection.

Candidate condition:

> A claimed participation must remain capable of becoming legible through
> projection.

This does not mean that every projection is immediately observed.

It preserves the distinction between:

    not projected

and:

    projected but not observed.

Therefore absence of observation does not by itself establish absence of
participation.

The distinction belongs to epistemology.

---

## 11. Candidate Contract: Capability and Ownership

Relationship:

    capability ──owned-by──> actor / participant

    capability ──consumed-by──> participant(s)

Candidate conditions:

> Canonical ownership of a capability is unique.

and:

> Capability ownership is distinct from capability consumption.

Therefore:

    one canonical owner
          │
          └── unlimited consumption

where permitted by the surrounding constitutional and relational
conditions.

Ownership does not create capability.

Capability does not require ownership in order to be possible.

The epistemology of ownership remains a separate concern.

---

## 12. Contract Does Not Cause Becoming

Contracts do not cause becoming.

EOS does not cause becoming.

IGE does not cause becoming.

OCS does not cause becoming.

OW does not cause becoming.

Actors do not own becoming.

Becoming is not a function performed by a framework.

The current EOS understanding is:

> Becoming is the continued existence of difference and transformation
> across the ecosystem.

Frameworks, actors, systems, relationships, capabilities, and states
persist within becoming.

They do not possess constitutional authority over becoming itself.

Contracts therefore preserve coherence **within becoming**.

They do not govern becoming as an object.

---

## 13. Contract and Survival

The purpose of contracts is not to prevent change.

A contract that requires a relationship to remain identical through all
change would prevent becoming rather than preserve coherence.

The contract boundary therefore concerns:

    coherent change

rather than:

    permanent sameness

EOS must remain capable of surviving change without losing the
distinctions that constitute its relationships.

Therefore:

> Contract preserves the conditions of coherent continuation, not
> immobility.

---

## 14. Contract Failure

A contract may be:

- satisfied;
- violated;
- contradicted;
- unobservable;
- unresolved;
- inapplicable;
- incorrectly specified.

These states must not be collapsed.

In particular:

    unknown ≠ false

    unresolved ≠ violated

    unobserved ≠ absent

    changed ≠ incoherent

Epistemology determines how contract status may be established.

---

## 15. Contract Lifecycle

A contract may move through a lifecycle:

    proposed
       ↓
    investigated
       ↓
    supported
       ↓
    constituted
       ↓
    applied
       ↓
    challenged
       ↓
    reconciled
       ↓
    retained / revised / rejected

No contract becomes authoritative merely because it has been proposed.

No implementation becomes authoritative merely because it implements a
proposed contract.

Contract authority must derive from the constitutional and relational
domain in which the contract exists.

---

## 16. Locality of Contracts

EOS contracts are local to EOS unless an IGE-level contract establishes
otherwise.

EOS may discover a contract that appears to have broader applicability.

That discovery does not automatically make it an IGE contract.

Likewise, an IGE contract may establish a citizenship condition for EOS
without determining EOS's complete local contract system.

Therefore:

    IGE contract
         ↓
    citizenship condition
         ↓
        EOS
         ↓
    EOS local contracts

The local contract system remains EOS's constitutional responsibility.

---

## 17. Non-Dictatorial Boundary

No EOS contract may claim authority merely because it is useful.

No EOS contract may establish authority over another IGE citizen merely
because the other citizen participates in a relationship with EOS.

No contract may silently expand its domain of authority beyond the
relationship that legitimizes it.

Therefore:

> Contract authority is bounded by the relationship and constitutional
> domain from which the contract derives.

This preserves:

    conformance ≠ obedience

    citizenship ≠ subordination

    difference ≠ incoherence

---

## 18. Contract Test

Before a statement becomes an EOS contract, test it:

1. What relationship does the statement govern?
2. What must remain true?
3. Why must it remain true?
4. What would falsify it?
5. Is the condition constitutional, relational, epistemic, or
   implementation-specific?
6. Is the condition local to EOS?
7. Does the condition preserve coherence or merely prescribe behavior?
8. Can the condition survive legitimate change?
9. What evidence could establish whether it holds?
10. What authority gives EOS the right to establish it?

If these questions cannot be answered, the statement remains a
hypothesis, relationship description, or open question.

---

## 19. Current Status

The following are contract candidates:

1. Identity requires maintained distinction.
2. Possibility must not be collapsed into capability.
3. Participation requires a distinguishable basis of agency.
4. Participation must remain capable of legible projection.
5. Canonical capability ownership is unique.
6. Ownership is distinct from consumption.
7. Contract conditions preserve coherent continuation rather than
   permanent sameness.

These are not yet EOS canon.

They remain subject to falsification, reconciliation, and epistemic
qualification.

---

## 20. Open Questions

1. Can every EOS relationship have a contract?
2. Can a relationship legitimately exist without a contract?
3. Can one relationship have multiple contracts?
4. Can contracts conflict?
5. Who or what resolves conflicting EOS contracts?
6. Can contracts emerge from relationships rather than being declared?
7. Can a contract itself participate in a relationship?
8. Can a contract become obsolete while the relationship remains valid?
9. What distinguishes an EOS contract from an IGE contract?
10. What is the minimum contract necessary for EOS citizenship?
11. Can an EOS contract constrain EOS itself?
12. Can a contract preserve possibility without defining the possibility
    space?

---

## 21. Current Semantic Boundary

The current EOS framework therefore distinguishes:

    CONSTITUTION
        what establishes legitimate local order

    ONTOLOGY
        what can exist

    RELATIONSHIPS
        how existing things relate

    CONTRACTS
        what must remain true for those relationships to remain coherent

    EPISTEMOLOGY
        how we know whether those conditions hold

    CONFORMANCE
        how EOS remains a legitimate IGE citizen

These are distinct domains.

None is reducible to the implementation.

None should be collapsed merely because an implementation expresses
several domains through the same software component.

---

## Final Principle

> A contract does not prevent becoming.
>
> A contract preserves the coherence necessary for continued becoming.
