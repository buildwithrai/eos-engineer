# Experiment-01 — F1 Identity vs Role

## Purpose

Attempt to falsify Discovery-01's identity resolution.

Determine whether EOS is a distinct identity or a role any existing
participant can exhibit.

---

## Attempt

Model EOS as a role assigned to each constitutional participant in turn.

For each model, check whether the participant can exhibit probabilistic
engineering judgment — evidence-gated, recorded as declared/candidate
state — without violating its constitutional responsibility.

---

## Models

### Model A — IGE plays EOS

IGE's reconciler contract requires: Deterministic, Idempotent,
Stateless, Replaceable, Observable.

"Reconcilers never invent information."

"Every output shall trace directly to observed repository facts."

Probabilistic judgment creates state that is not traceable to observed
facts. It is, by definition, invented.

Assigning judgment to IGE violates the Reconciler Contract.

### Model B — OCS plays EOS

OCS's responsibility is Observation.

"Observation never owns the observed."

OCS ADR-0001 binds OCS to a canonical representation from which all
outputs derive deterministically.

OCS's own handbook: "Deterministic Before Probabilistic." AI augments,
does not replace.

Assigning probabilistic judgment to OCS crosses from measuring to
deciding. It is ownership-adjacent and violates the observation
contract.

### Model C — Omnia plays EOS

Omnia's responsibility is Production: produce organizational reality.

Judgment is not production. Producing judgment does not produce
organizational reality.

### Model D — Corpus plays EOS

Corpus's responsibility is Sustainment: sustain validated discoveries,
experiments, and relationships.

Judgment is not sustainment. Sustaining knowledge does not judge it.

---

## Success

Every assignment of EOS as a role violates the participant's
constitutional responsibility.

EOS is not a role. EOS survives as a distinct identity.

---

## Failure

Any participant exhibits probabilistic engineering judgment without
violating its constitutional responsibility.

EOS collapses to a role.

---

## Result

Models A-D each require the participant to violate its constitutional
responsibility.

No participant can be EOS without ceasing to be itself.

EOS is a distinct identity, not a role.

## Status

Not falsified.
